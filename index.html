Professional Profile

Humanitarian Evidence at the Intersection of Data and Responsibility

I am a humanitarian MEAL professional with nine years of progressive field and headquarters experience across Iraq, Ukraine, Hungary, Romania, Greece, and Latvia. My work has spanned the full spectrum of monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning, from conducting protection monitoring interviews with displaced families during the Mosul military operation to leading headquarters level MEAL strategy across multicountry, multidonor portfolios.

I have built MEAL systems from the ground up, led independent evaluations aligned with OECD DAC criteria, designed feedback and accountability mechanisms under the Core Humanitarian Standard, managed teams of up to 28 staff across multiple field sites, and generated evidence that informed programme decisions for donors including ECHO, USAID, UNICEF, UNHCR, GIZ, SDC, and the EU.

I am currently completing an MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Smart Systems (Big Data analytics) at Budapest University of Technology and Economics. This is not a career change. It is a deliberate expansion. The humanitarian sector's most pressing data governance challenges are increasingly shaped by AI: automated targeting, predictive analytics, algorithmic decision making in protection sensitive contexts. I bring the field experience to understand what responsible adoption must look like in practice.

Responsible AI & Humanitarian Technology

From Data Governance to AI Governance

For nine years I have worked at the centre of humanitarian data: designing the systems that collect it, the frameworks that govern its quality, the accountability mechanisms that ensure affected populations have a voice in how it is used, and the evidence processes that translate it into programme decisions.

I am now completing an MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Smart Systems (Big Data analytics) at Budapest University of Technology and Economics, studying how machine learning, data analytics, and intelligent systems work, so that I can bring technical understanding to the governance and adoption questions the humanitarian sector must answer.

I do not approach AI as a technologist looking for humanitarian applications. I approach it as a humanitarian professional who understands the operational, ethical, and protection realities of data use in crisis contexts, and who is building the technical literacy to lead responsible adoption.

Data Governance in Practice

Nine years of responsible data management, data quality assurance, data protection practices, and CHS aligned accountability in contexts where data directly affects vulnerable populations.

Accountability Frameworks

Designed and implemented Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanisms (FCRM) and accountability systems aligned with CHS, AAP, Sphere, and Do No Harm. The same governance principles that must inform AI adoption.

Evidence Based Decision Making

Led evaluations, needs assessments, and evidence generation processes that informed how humanitarian programmes adapt. The evidence culture that should govern how AI tools are tested and validated.

Formal AI Education

MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Smart Systems (Big Data analytics), Budapest University of Technology and Economics (2024 to 2026), building technical understanding of machine learning, data analytics, and intelligent systems.
